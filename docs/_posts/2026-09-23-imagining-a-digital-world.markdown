Digi-Terra is an homage to fictional computer worlds popular in sci-fi properties of the 80s, 90s, and early 2000s. Movies like [_Tron_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tron) and television shows like [_ReBoot_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ReBoot) imagined hidden worlds inside a computer where programs and algorithms exist as sentient beings, while other properties like [_The Hacker and the Ants_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hacker_and_the_Ants), [_VR Troopers_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VR_Troopers), [_MegaMan NT Warrior_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MegaMan_NT_Warrior), and [_Otherland_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otherland) depicted highly-advanced virtual realities with which living humans interface via computer peripherals.

As such, Digi-Terra is inspired by a long lineage of science-fiction originating in the [cyberpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberpunk) subgenre. However my vision for the project is closest to the canadian television series _ReBoot_ and the Disney file _Tron_. _ReBoot_ inspires the visual aesthetic---a brightly-colored cyberspace resembling natural and man-made objects from the real world. _Tron_ inspires the narrative premise---users and programmers immersing themselves in a virtual world inside the computer.

I intend to work _with_ the standard tropes of _Luanti_ and other mining/survival game rather than against them, so its necessary to contextualize some of the objects in the game.

## Nodes and Blocks

At its core _Luanti_ works with two fundamental types of blocks, namely "water" and "stone", plus a pseudo-node representing emptiness usually called "air". In the cyberspace of Digi-Terra water blocks represent a kind of "RAM". The uniformity of sea-level is the RAM's neutral state, while the stone blocks rising out of it and extending below the surface represent "bits", the negative and positive nature of information stored in RAM. Air is another neutral space where programs and algorithms can move around freely while they manipulate data the data in and out of RAM. Entering a node of water (or stone) restricts a program's movement and causes suffocation due to the zeros and ones damaging the program's digital structure.

## The Sun, the Moon, and Stars

Since _Luanti_ has a day-night cycle, the motion of the sun and the moon through the sky represent the CPU's clock cycle. Its a kind of on-off cycle that represents deterministic progress through time. Without the clock cycle programs would be motionless, but programs are unaware of any change in the passage of time if the computer is off, suspended, or throttled.

The stars in the sky represent CPU voltages slowly winking out during the "off" cycle.

## Clouds and the Blue Sky

Clouds are constantly drifting through the sky while you're playing _Luanti_. In the world of Digi-Terra these represend "freed" memory on its way to garbage collection. The color of the sky is a result of memory allocation and deallocation moving constantly back-and-forth to manage active RAM.

## Fog and Other Stuff

Fog is the limit of each program's awareness of the local state of RAM. When implemented, trees will probably represent branching logic algorithms (if-then-else, switch-case, etc.).

## I Know This is Unserious

Directly mapping the visual appearance of _Luanti_, or any virtual world for that matter, to actual processes inside a computer is not a focus of this project. The explanations above are meant to generate an _aesthetic_ much in the way _ReBoot_ and _Tron_ casually drop references to "programs", "sprites", "glitches", and "the Net".

"Tron" is the name of a character/program in the movie _Tron_, but the word comes from a debugging technique known as "Trace On". "Sprites" are a word to describe visually-represented individuals inside the computers of _ReBoot_, but the word comes from a graphical technique used to program video games. None of it makes real sense because its meant to establish a feeling. Its even a little bit whimsical.

Characters in the Digi-Terra universe will have similar origins. For instance [Mallo is a martial artist](https://opengameart.org/content/neko-cat-fighter-animated-jason-em-style) named after the C library "malloc" (Memory ALLOCate).

## The Aesthetic

It would be remiss of me not to mention the influence of [OCD](https://github.com/bas080/OCD_nodes), a _Minetest_ game started by [Bas80 in 2012](https://forum.luanti.org/viewtopic.php?p=265181) and [continued by octacian in 2015](https://github.com/octacian/ocd2). The initial textures for Digi-Terra are lifted directly from the OCD texture pack. The smooth textures of OCD provide Digi-Terra with an interesting visual consistency to make it stand out from standard _Minecraft_ clones.
